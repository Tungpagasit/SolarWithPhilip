Decision framework

Four Ways Homeowners Manage Energy Costs

The goal is not to force a winner. It is to understand how each path handles cost, control, responsibility and risk.

1. Continue With the Utility

May fit: very low usage, a property with inadequate solar access, or a homeowner who intentionally accepts future utility pricing and wants no home-energy changes now.

Benefits: no solar agreement, installation or equipment responsibility.

Tradeoffs: future rates and fees remain outside the homeowner’s control; no onsite generation or battery resilience.

2. Cash Purchase

May fit: homeowners who prioritize immediate ownership and have capital available.

Benefits: immediate ownership, no financing payment, long-term control.

Tradeoffs: large upfront capital commitment, ownership responsibility and opportunity cost of the funds used.

3. Financed Ownership

May fit: ownership-focused homeowners who prefer payments over a cash purchase.

Benefits: ownership without paying the full price at installation.

Tradeoffs: interest and financing costs, ownership responsibility, and potentially high total cost depending on rate and term.

4. Solar Lease or PPA

May fit: homeowners focused on low upfront cost, predictable cash flow, service coverage, batteries and preserving capital.

Benefits: access to solar without purchasing the system, provider responsibilities defined by contract, and potential sale or end-of-term options.

Tradeoffs: a long-term agreement, transfer and buyout provisions that must be understood, and no homeowner ownership during the initial term.
