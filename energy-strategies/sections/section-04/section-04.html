Solar 101

Follow the energy from sunlight to the home, battery and grid.

Solar + storage is easier to evaluate once the homeowner can see where the electricity comes from, where it goes, and what the grid still does.

1Sunlight, roof direction and production

Solar produces electricity only while sunlight reaches the panels. Production changes through the day and falls to zero at night. Shade, roof direction, roof pitch, usable area and roof condition all affect the result.

South-facing roof planesOften provide strong annual solar exposure in California.
East-facing roof planesFavor more morning production.
West-facing roof planesFavor more afternoon production and can be strategically useful when later-day electricity is expensive.
The practical design questionNot “How many panels fit?” but “How much useful production can this roof deliver for this home?”
2Follow the electricity through the home

When solar is producing, the home uses available solar electricity first. If the home needs more electricity than solar is producing, the difference must come from available battery energy or the utility grid.

Solar → HomeActive household loads use solar directly.
Extra Solar → BatterySurplus production can charge storage for later.
Extra After That → GridRemaining surplus may be exported under the applicable utility tariff.
Grid → HomeThe grid supplies electricity when solar and available battery energy are not enough.
3Charge the battery before expecting it to serve the night

The solar array has to serve daytime household loads and still produce enough surplus to charge the battery. A home that consumes most of its solar production during the day may have less energy available to store.

Solar-array productionMust be evaluated together with daytime household consumption.
Battery capacityDetermines how much energy can be stored, but usable capacity is not the same thing as unlimited backup.
Operating reserveA self-consumption strategy may keep part of the battery available for backup, depending on homeowner priorities and equipment settings.
Backup powerAlso depends on which circuits are selected and the battery's output capability, not just the battery's kWh rating.
Design lesson: An undersized solar array or heavy daytime usage can prevent the battery from reaching the charge level the homeowner expects for the evening or an outage.
4Understand imports, exports, the bill and True-Up

The utility meter measures electricity imported from and exported to the grid. Solar electricity that is produced and used immediately inside the home can reduce an import without first becoming an export.

ImportElectricity the home buys from PG&E when onsite sources are not covering the load.
ExportSurplus electricity sent to the grid after onsite use and charging needs.
Legacy NEM customersAnnual charges and credits have historically been reconciled at True-Up under their applicable tariff.
Usage growth mattersIf household consumption rises while solar production does not, remaining grid purchases can rise too.

Net Billing customers are billed differently from legacy NEM customers, so the actual PG&E tariff and statement control.

5Set realistic grid and grandfathering expectations

A typical solar + battery home remains connected to the utility grid. Solar does not automatically make every grid purchase, utility charge or future bill disappear.

The grid still mattersIt can supply electricity when household demand exceeds available solar and stored energy.
Connection-related chargesApplicable utility charges can remain even when grid purchases are substantially reduced.
InterconnectionEstablishes the project's applicable utility treatment and authorization process.
GrandfatheringThe applicable period and conditions depend on the governing tariff and customer eligibility. Do not assume every solar customer has the same rules.
NEM 3 / Net Billing

What changed, why it changed, and how a good design responds

WHEN

The CPUC adopted the Net Billing Tariff on December 15, 2022. It applies to new applicable PG&E, SCE and SDG&E interconnection applications submitted on or after April 15, 2023.

WHY

The tariff values exported electricity according to grid value and was designed to encourage storage and shifting energy into later hours, including periods when the grid needs electricity more.

WHAT CHANGED

Solar used directly onsite avoids an import. Excess electricity exported to the grid is credited based on avoided-cost value rather than being treated like the full retail electricity the homeowner may buy later.

THE PG&E DESIGN PROBLEM

A home can export electricity during lower-value daytime periods and then buy electricity from PG&E after solar production falls. Designing only around annual production can miss that timing problem.

The solar + battery design response

1. Use solar directlyServe active household loads while the array is producing.
2. Create useful surplusProduce enough appropriate surplus to charge storage after daytime loads.
3. Store itUse battery capacity and reserve settings that match the homeowner's goals.
4. Reduce later importsDischarge stored energy when the home would otherwise buy grid electricity after solar production declines.

What must be designed correctly: roof exposure, hourly consumption, expected EV/HVAC/ADU loads, battery capacity and power, reserve setting, backup circuits, rate plan and remaining utility charges.

The Practical Next Step

Why solar + battery is the practical next step

For many California homes, rooftop solar is the broadly adaptable onsite electricity source. Battery storage makes the system more useful under current import/export economics by moving solar energy into later hours and adding selected resilience.

  • Serve daytime household loads with onsite production.
  • Store appropriate surplus instead of relying only on exports.
  • Reduce higher-cost grid imports after solar production falls.
  • Design backup around the circuits and duration that actually matter to the homeowner.

Solar + battery does not automatically eliminate the utility bill, and it still has to be designed, priced and structured correctly.

Now that the energy system makes sense, the next question is not whether sunlight works.It is which financial and responsibility structure best matches what you want solved.
