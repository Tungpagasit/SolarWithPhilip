Understand The Available Energy Sources

What is realistically available for a home?

A quick reality check before we spend time comparing solar choices.

Utility / Grid Electricity

The default option is to keep buying electricity from the utility.

Why it worksAlready connected. No onsite energy project is required.
Why homeowners look beyond itFuture rates, tariffs and time-of-use rules remain outside the homeowner's control. Added electric loads normally increase grid purchases.
Explore Energy Costs →

Residential Wind

Small wind can produce renewable electricity, but it needs a property with a verified wind resource and enough room for the equipment.

Best fitSpecialized windy sites with suitable space, setbacks and local approval.
Reality checkUsually less practical for ordinary suburban lots and rooftops.

Ground-Source / Geothermal

Ground-source heat pumps can make heating and cooling more efficient by exchanging heat with the ground.

StrengthEfficient HVAC where a ground loop is practical.
Reality checkIt requires significant site work and does not itself generate the home's electricity.

Solar + Storage

Solar produces electricity at the home. Storage can move daytime solar into later hours and can be configured for backup.

Why it fits ordinary homesUses available roof space, scales to household usage and pairs onsite generation with storage.
What still mattersRoof, shade, electrical conditions, system design, usage timing, interconnection and actual contract terms.
Adoption and maintenance: Rooftop solar is the only one of these onsite electricity options deployed at broad residential scale in California. This section is a practical screening step, not an apples-to-apples maintenance-cost comparison.

Energy-source takeaway

The grid remains part of most home strategies. For ordinary California homes that want onsite electricity production, solar is the practical option to evaluate further, and storage can change when that solar energy is used and how backup is designed.
