Why 100% annual offset can still leave a meaningful True-Up

Annual offset adds twelve months together, but the home does not produce and use electricity evenly. Summer solar production is typically stronger, so the home may fill the battery and still export excess energy at a relatively low value. Winter brings shorter production days and longer periods of household demand. A battery can shift energy from daytime into the evening, but it cannot store July's surplus for December.

That is why the planning goal is not simply “make annual solar equal annual usage.” A higher annual-production scenario can reduce the winter import gap while summer credits help offset part of the remaining imported electricity. The 160% case is shown as a planning scenario, not a promise that True-Up or utility charges will be eliminated.
