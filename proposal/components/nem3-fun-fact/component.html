Fun Fact: One Rule Changed the Way California Solar WorksOpen the story
natural monopoly

California’s new solar billing rules began applying to new interconnection applications on April 15, 2023.

Before that change, California solar was commonly designed around a simpler idea: produce electricity during the day, send the extra to the grid, and receive credits much closer to the retail value of electricity used later.

The Net Billing Tariff (NEM 3) changed that relationship. Daytime exports are now valued according to when the electricity reaches the grid, and many of those exports are worth far less than the electricity the homeowner purchases later.

Suddenly, producing more electricity was not enough. When and where that electricity was used became critical.

Battery storage helped create a new strategy: solar serves the home first, available surplus charges the battery, the battery helps serve the home later, and only the remaining electricity goes to the grid.

A battery cannot store solar energy that was never produced. Summer may provide abundant charging opportunities, while winter and lower-sun months may leave less surplus available.
